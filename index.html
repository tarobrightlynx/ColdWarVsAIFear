How the study worked

The design combines historical document collection, automated contemporary-source discovery, repeated LLM coding, and source-level statistical comparison.

STEP 01

Collect documents

60 Cold War sources were assembled manually. Two AI datasets of 120 sources each were collected for January–June 2025 and 2026.

STEP 02

Scrape AI sources

A BigQuery workflow used GDELT public data to identify and download recent AI-related articles.

STEP 03

Apply fixed coding prompt

GPT-4o mini rated overall fear from 1–10 and assigned one or more of ten fear categories.

STEP 04

Repeat 25 times

Each source was coded repeatedly and averaged to treat output variation as model uncertainty.

STEP 05

Compare groups

Source-level averages were summarized and compared with independent-sample Welch t-tests.

Fear-intensity scale

1 = little or no fear expressed

10 = catastrophic language, urgent existential danger, or fear approaching irrationality

Human validation

Ten percent of collected documents were reviewed by a human to check model accuracy. The paper notes that future work should add formal intercoder-reliability testing.

What the pattern means

The research argues that AI fear is not simply weaker nuclear fear. It is a different structure of anxiety.

Nuclear fear is concentrated.It centers on visible physical destruction, radiation, global war, mass death, and civilization-level collapse.
AI fear is diffuse.It spreads across jobs, surveillance, media trust, infrastructure, creativity, governance, and automated decision-making.
The central shift is control.The key concern moves from whether humans will survive technology to whether humans will remain in charge of it.

Data quality and interpretation notes

These cautions are important for accurately reading the dashboard and the underlying paper.

Figure 6 inconsistency

The Figure 6 caption says the 2025–2026 mean difference was statistically significant with p = 7.90e−05. However, Table 2 and the surrounding Results text report p = .946 and describe the difference as not significant. This dashboard follows Table 2 and the surrounding narrative.

Instability p-value should be checked

Geopolitical Instability rises from .181 in 2025 to .383 in 2026, yet Table 2 reports p = .887. Because that combination appears unusual, the raw test output should be verified before publication.

Source collections differ

Cold War sources include speeches, government documents, newspapers, and memoirs. AI sources are primarily modern digital articles and reports, so genre may affect tone.

Selection and access limits

BigQuery and scraping can miss paywalled, blocked, incomplete, or script-rendered pages. The collected AI corpus may not represent every important discussion.

LLM coding is interpretive

Repeated model runs reduce instability but do not replace full human coding. Borderline categories and historical language can still be misread.

Document is the unit of analysis

The strongest comparisons should use source-level averages rather than treating all repeated model runs as independent observations.
